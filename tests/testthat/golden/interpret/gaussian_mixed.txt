## model
A gaussian model of y, fitted to 240 observations. Observations are grouped by site, and that grouping is modelled, so the estimates below already allow for it.

## effects
x: very strong evidence (p < 0.001) that x is associated with y. Across the middle half of x, predicted y is 0.90 at -0.871 against 1.85 at 0.678: 0.945 higher (95% interval 0.757 to 1.134). That is 0.611 per unit of x.
z: little evidence (p = 0.243) that z is associated with y. Across the middle half of z, predicted y is 1.34 at -0.764 against 1.45 at 0.431: 0.103 higher (95% interval from 0.070 lower to 0.275 higher). That is 0.0860 per unit of z. The interval includes zero, which means the data are consistent with no effect -- not that there is none.
h3: strong evidence (p = 0.00144) that h3 is associated with y. Predicted y is 1.59 for 'north', 1.55 for 'south' and 1.04 for 'west'.

## diagnostics
All 10 fitting checks passed: the optimiser converged, the gradient is at zero and the information matrix is usable. These say the fit is sound, not that the model is right -- for that, run ilm_appraise().

## caveats
These are associations. Nothing here rules out a common cause of a predictor and the response, so an effect could differ in size or sign from what is reported. To say more you need a design that identifies one: ilm_dag_model() with an adjustment set, ilm_did(), ilm_rdd().
The tests above use a large-sample reference, and with 24 groups of `site` a test of an effect that varies between groups can run somewhat liberal. For a term the conclusions rest on, ilm_pb_lrt() calibrates the p-value by simulation, and ilm_denom_df() gives finite degrees of freedom.
