## model
A poisson model of k, fitted to 240 observations. Observations are grouped by site, and that grouping is modelled, so the estimates below already allow for it. The model works on the log scale, so its coefficients are not in counts; the effects below are converted. They hold the random effect of site at zero -- a typical site -- rather than averaging over site; on the log scale the two are not the same.

## effects
x: very strong evidence (p < 0.001) that x is associated with k. Across the middle half of x, predicted k is 1.16 at -0.871 against 2.00 at 0.678: 0.85 higher (95% interval 0.61 to 1.08). (Rate ratio 1.43 per unit of x.)
g2: moderate evidence (p = 0.0317) that g2 is associated with k. Predicted k is 1.501 for 'control' and 1.864 for 'treated': 0.363 higher for 'treated' (95% interval 0.027 to 0.700). (Rate ratio 1.24 for 'treated' against 'control'.)

## diagnostics
All 11 fitting checks passed: the optimiser converged, the gradient is at zero and the information matrix is usable. These say the fit is sound, not that the model is right -- for that, run ilm_appraise().

## caveats
These are associations. Nothing here rules out a common cause of a predictor and the response, so an effect could differ in size or sign from what is reported. To say more you need a design that identifies one: ilm_dag_model() with an adjustment set, ilm_did(), ilm_rdd().
The effects above, like the coefficients, are for a typical group. Predictions averaged over the groups instead come from predict(groups = "population"), and the effects from ilm_ame(groups = "population").
The tests above use a large-sample reference, and with 24 groups of `site` a test of an effect that varies between groups can run somewhat liberal. For a term the conclusions rest on, ilm_pb_lrt() calibrates the p-value by simulation.
