### What holds

illume's intervals cover better than mclogit's in 6 of 6 regimes; the widest gaps are rare (0.931 against 0.853), combined (0.945 against 0.894).
mclogit shrinks fixed effects (attenuation below 0.95) in 5 of 6 regimes, from 0.816 to 0.918.
illume is 6 to 13 times faster, by median seconds per fit.

### Findings that go AGAINST illume, and must travel with the rest

- illume INFLATES coefficients (attenuation above 1.1) in rare (1.462 against 0.816), combined (1.301 against 0.918).
- illume's mean absolute bias is higher than mclogit's in rare (0.394 against 0.271), flat_re (0.012 against 0.008), combined (0.370 against 0.368).
- illume's fits were usable less often than mclogit's in rare (0.973 against 1.000), flat_re (0.632 against 1.000), combined (0.910 against 1.000); its coverage there is CONDITIONAL on the usable fits.
- With a true random-effect SD of 0.05 neither method recovers it: illume reports 0.099 and mclogit 0.004.
- mclogit's RMSE is LOWER in 4 of 6 regimes (clean, unbalanced, flat_re, heavy_re), by 3 to 14%.

CAVEAT that must travel with this result: the two methods fail
differently rather than one dominating. PQL converges, shrinks, and does
not say so. The Laplace approximation's intervals mean what they claim,
and it declines to answer more often -- loudly, which is the intent, but
a table conditional on the fits that were usable hides that cost.
